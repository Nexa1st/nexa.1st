## Start with the workflow, not the tools

Tool-hopping is the fastest way to publish nothing. A short-form AI workflow has five jobs — idea, generation, edit, caption, publish — and you only need one reliable tool per job.

## The stack, job by job

**Ideation and scripts.** A general AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — see the Tools Directory) is enough for hooks, titles and shot lists. Keep a series bible: character descriptions, locations and rules, pasted into every session so output stays consistent.

**Generation.** Use whichever generator your current project is built on, and learn its real limits: clip length, reference-image rules, and whether free use is capped. Free lanes change their rules often — verify on the official site before planning a batch around them.

**Editing.** CapCut covers fast vertical edits and captions for most creators; DaVinci Resolve is the step up for colour and finishing. OBS Studio handles recording and streaming at no cost.

**Design.** Canva covers thumbnails and posters. Keep one template per series so your feed looks intentional.

**Publishing notes.** A simple notes system — Notion or even a plain document — tracking what was posted, where, and what is still scheduled beats any expensive dashboard at this scale.

## What "free" honestly means

Free tiers usually trade something: a daily or total generation cap, a watermark on some tools, lower resolution, or a queue. None of that blocks starting. It does mean you should:

- Batch your generations instead of wasting them on unplanned tests.
- Keep a local archive of every finished file.
- Never build a business promise on a free tier you have not re-checked this month.

## The bottom line

You do not need twelve subscriptions. You need one tool per job, a series bible, and the discipline to finish and post. Upgrade only the step that is measurably slowing you down.
