## The opening frame is the whole pitch

On a fast feed, a viewer decides before your story starts. So do not start the story — start the payoff's question. The first visual must make someone think: *what is happening here?*

## Five hook patterns that work for AI video

1. **Impossible scale** — something far too big or too small for its setting, visible immediately.
2. **Mid-action freeze of logic** — the clip opens already in motion: a chase, a fall, a transformation half-done.
3. **Wrong place** — a familiar creature or object in a believable everyday location.
4. **Before/after in one shot** — the change happens inside the first two seconds.
5. **A face reacting** — a character's shocked reaction aimed at something just off-frame pulls the eye with it.

## Build the clip backwards

Write the final twist first, then the hook that promises it. A simple 15-second map: 0–2s the hook, 2–6s the situation becomes clear, 6–11s escalation, 11–15s payoff, twist, or a loop back to the opening frame.

## Sound is part of the hook

A strong, matched sound effect in the opening moment raises retention more than background music left at its default start. When you do add music, shift it so its strongest part lands on your video's strongest moment.

## Test honestly

Post, then read the numbers you actually have — views, watch time, shares — in your platform's own analytics. A hook is only "strong" when real viewers stayed. Never call a format guaranteed viral; call it tested.
