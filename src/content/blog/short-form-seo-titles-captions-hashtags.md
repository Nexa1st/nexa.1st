## Your clip is judged by its text before it is watched

Platforms cannot watch your video the way a person does. They read the title, caption, hashtags, on-screen context and early viewer behaviour. Strong text does not rescue a weak clip — but weak text buries a strong one.

## Titles: one clear promise

Write one search-friendly title per finished piece. Put the subject and the twist in plain words a viewer would actually type: the creature, the place, the event. Save the mystery for the video; the title's job is matching intent.

## Captions: unique, every time

Reusing one caption block across a whole series trains the platform to treat your posts as duplicates. Write a fresh caption per clip — what happens, a question viewers can answer in comments, and the series context. A good comment question is genuine engagement fuel: ask the choice the story itself raises.

## Hashtags: a small, relevant set

Use a small set of hashtags that describe this clip: a couple of broad discovery tags plus specific ones for the subject and series. Tags that do not match the content may win a click and lose the viewer — and watch time is the signal that compounds.

## The pinned comment

On platforms that allow it, pin a first comment that adds something: the question, the next part's promise, or the link hub. Prepare it when you prepare the post, not as an afterthought.

## Measure, then repeat what worked

Check each platform's analytics weekly. Which titles got search traffic? Which openings held viewers? Repeat winning structures with new subjects — that is the entire "algorithm strategy": tested patterns, honestly measured.
