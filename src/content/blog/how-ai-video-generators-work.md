## The short version

An AI video generator does not film anything. It predicts what a sequence of frames should look like, starting from your prompt — and, when you provide one, a reference image that anchors who or what is in the scene. Modern models build motion, lighting and camera movement together, which is why the same prompt can produce a cinematic shot on one run and a strange one on the next.

That unpredictability is not a flaw you can fully remove. It is the workflow: generate, inspect every frame, regenerate only what failed.

## What your prompt really controls

A good prompt is a shot description, not a wish. The parts that matter most:

- **Subject** — exactly who or what, described the same way every time for series work.
- **Action** — one clear physical action. Effects and spectacle land better when the physical action comes first and the effect follows it.
- **Camera** — a motivated move: slow push-in, low tracking shot, static locked frame.
- **Light and place** — believable locations with believable light beat abstract "epic" wording.
- **Duration discipline** — most short-form generators work in clips of a few seconds. Plan a story as a sequence of complete clips, not one long scene.

## Why reference images change everything

For recurring characters, a reference image is an identity lock. Face, hairstyle, outfit and proportions stay recognisable across scenes because the model starts from the reference instead of inventing a new person each time. Without one, "the same character" is a request the model can only guess at.

Some free generators accept only one reference image per generation. When that is your limit, design scenes around a single anchor character and describe the rest in text.

## The two-second rule

Short-form platforms reward the opening, not the build-up. Put the impossible thing in the first frame: the creature, the crash, the transformation. A reliable structure for a 10–15 second clip is hook in 0–2 seconds, build from 2–6, escalation from 6–11, and a payoff, twist or loop at the end.

## A quality checklist that saves credits

1. Play the clip before downloading it — never trust a thumbnail.
2. Check faces, hands and text in individual frames.
3. Check that the clip is actually your scene and not another generation.
4. Regenerate a failed scene once with a clearer prompt — not endlessly.
5. Keep the audio claim honest: a file having an audio track is not proof the sound matches the action.

## The bottom line

AI video is a directing job now. The creators who win are not the ones with the longest prompts — they are the ones with locked characters, honest quality checks, and openings that refuse to be scrolled past.
