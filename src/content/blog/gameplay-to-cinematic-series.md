## A clip is content; a series is an asset

One great clip gets views. A series gets followers, because every episode markets the next. The difference is continuity — and continuity is a checklist, not a talent.

## Lock these before episode one

- **Characters** — exact appearance, outfit and abilities, from a reference you reuse every time.
- **World rules** — what is possible, what it costs, what never happens.
- **Visual signature** — aspect ratio, colour language, and one recognisable motif.
- **Story position** — every episode opens from the previous episode's final beat. No resets.

## Episode structure that retains

Open on the strongest moment you have, escalate once, and end on a question the next episode answers. For longer stories, build in logical parts and release them on a predictable schedule your audience can learn — same time, same format.

## Production discipline

Generate scene by scene, verify each clip in a player before accepting it, and keep a simple ledger: scene number, prompt used, status, and where the file lives. When a generation fails or returns the wrong scene, that ledger is the difference between a recovered day and a lost one.

## Titles and packaging

Give the complete saga one strong search title, and each part its own caption and hashtags. Package is part of the product: a viewer who cannot tell part 3 from part 7 will not binge either.

## The bottom line

Lock the characters, respect the story position, verify every scene, and end every episode owing the viewer an answer. That is how gameplay becomes a franchise.
