Mastershot Studio is a free video editor that runs in your browser, and its free plan makes a promise almost no other online editor makes: exports in up to 4K, with no watermark, forever, for $0, with no credit card, credits, or minutes to run out of. That headline is stated in plain words on the official homepage. But Mastershot Studio is an editor, not an AI video generator — it cannot turn a text prompt into a new video — and two things a creator needs to know are not answered on the official pages: whether free exports can be used commercially, and whether you need an account at all. This review follows the free plan as the official pages state it; everything else is marked not verified.

## What it is

Mastershot Studio is a free online video editor at studio.mastershot.app, from the same maker as the single-job tools at mastershot.app. It runs in Chrome, Edge, or Safari on a computer: drop in videos, photos, and music, trim and split clips on a timeline, add text, titles, music, and subtitles, then export once as an MP4.

The detail that shapes everything else is where the work happens. Your files open straight from your computer, so there is nothing to upload first: "A 2 GB video opens right away. It's read from your computer, not uploaded." The AI tools run on your computer too, instead of on Mastershot's servers, which is why the page says there are no credits or minutes to run out of and your video is not uploaded to use them.

The toolset covers the full basic job in one project instead of one tool at a time. The official page makes the comparison itself: online, a video often takes one site to trim, another to join, and a third to add music, downloading the video each time. In Studio, those are steps in a single project, and the video is saved once, at the end. Features include trim and split, combining clips and photos, speed from a quarter to four times, freeze frames and reverse, animated titles, transitions, filters, pan and zoom, music with volume and fades, voiceover recording, and audio cleanup.

The AI tools are part of the free editor, not a paid add-on. Studio writes subtitles timed to each word, in styles such as Bold or Highlight. Magic Cut finds long pauses and filler words, stripes them on the timeline, and lets you preview, untick anything to keep, and cut the rest in one go. Background removal takes out what is behind the person in a clip with no green screen. Face blur finds each person, follows them as they move, and lets you click a face to keep that person visible. These tools are free too, because they run on your computer.

There is also a no-time-limit screen recorder for Chrome. Beyond Studio, the main Mastershot site offers more than forty single-job tools — trim, compress, combine, crop, convert formats, and image and audio tools — free, with no account and no watermark on saved files, per the official homepage.

One limit: Studio needs a computer — the FAQ says it is not yet on phones, where Mastershot's other tools still trim, compress, and convert videos.

## Free plan details

The free plan is stated plainly on the official Studio homepage, read on 12 November 2026: "$0 for good", and the page's own summary is "The editor is free. Pro keeps your files for you." There is no daily or monthly cap stated anywhere — no credits, no minutes, no video-count limit. The page says it three ways: "No credits or minutes to run out of", "No credit card to start", and "Nothing gets locked when you click Export". The editor, the 4K exports, and the AI tools are free; the only thing money buys is storage and sharing.

Exports are where the free plan is most generous. The official page promises "Export in up to 4K" and "You get an MP4 with no watermark, in up to 4K, made on your computer", and the free plan's own bullet lists "4K export, subtitles, background removal and the other AI tools". Most free editors cap exports at 720p and charge for anything higher; here the top resolution is free. There is also no time limit: "Any length — no time limit on a video".

Aspect ratio is built for short-form creators: the editor switches to 9:16 for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube, or square. That matters because this is the tool you would use after generating clips elsewhere: cut AI-generated scenes together, add subtitles and music, crop to 9:16, and export in up to 4K — free, and with no watermark.

The one thing the official page answers two different ways is the account. The free plan's bullet says "Every video, image and audio tool, on your device, with no watermark and no account". But the FAQ, answering "Will I need an account?", says: "Yes. You sign in with Google or with an email and password. Your account keeps your projects, and the video files stay on your computer unless you have Pro." The closing section agrees with the FAQ. The page contradicts itself, so the account requirement is recorded here as not verified — expect to sign in, even though one bullet promises no account.

The paid ladder shows what the free plan's limits cost to remove — almost nothing, because the free plan has almost no limits. Mastershot Pro is $7 a month, or $70 a year ($5.83 a month with 2 months free). Pro adds three things only: a shareable link for every screen recording, opening any Studio project on another computer with its videos already there, and 100 GB for your recordings and project files. The page is explicit that "The editor itself is the same with or without it", and payments go through Polar. No paid tier unlocks a better editor, a higher resolution, or watermark removal — those are already free — so the upgrade is purely about cloud storage and sharing links.

## Watermark and commercial use

The watermark answer is the clearest statement on the whole official page, and it is worth quoting exactly. The FAQ asks whether exported videos will have a watermark, and answers: "No. Nothing you export from Studio has a watermark." The homepage says the same thing in its headline: "A free online video editor, with no watermark on anything you export." The single-job tools on mastershot.app carry the same promise: "no watermark on the files you save". A free export you can post as-is is rare — stated twice, in the headline and in the FAQ, with no exception listed for the free plan.

Commercial use is a different story. The word "commercial" does not appear anywhere on the official Studio homepage or the main Mastershot homepage as read on 12 November 2026, and no separate terms page for Mastershot surfaced in a search for its terms of service. That means whether you may use a free Studio export in a monetized video, a client project, or a paid ad is not verified. The tool edits your own footage, so the underlying clips are yours — but the licence for the edited output and the AI features is not stated on the pages a reader can check. Until Mastershot publishes a licence or terms statement, treat commercial use as not verified: fine for personal projects and tests, and a question to confirm with the maker before you build a monetized or client workflow on it. Do not assume that "free and no watermark" means "free for commercial use" — those are different promises, and only the first one is made here.

## How to use it

The free plan changes nothing about the sequence — only the two questions above, which you should settle before you post commercially.

First, open https://studio.mastershot.app in Chrome, Edge, or Safari on a computer. The page says to sign in with Google or with an email address, then add your videos — expect the sign-in step, because the FAQ and the closing section both say an account is required, even though one bullet says no account is needed. No credit card is asked for.

Second, start a project and drop in your videos, photos, and music — they open straight from your computer, so even a 2 GB video is ready right away, with no upload wait. Drag clips into order, trim their ends, press S to split at the playhead, and put text, pictures, and music on the rows above and below the main clips.

Third, do the finishing work in the same project. Switch to 9:16 for TikTok, Reels, or Shorts. Let Studio write the subtitles from the speech, fix any wrong word, and pick a style. Run Magic Cut to strip pauses and filler words, previewing first and unticking anything to keep. Remove a background or blur faces where the shot needs it, add music with a fade, or record a voiceover as the video plays.

Fourth, export once. Pick a size — up to 4K — and click Export for an MP4 with no watermark, made on your computer. Check the file before you post it: no watermark, the resolution you asked for, and the cuts where you expected. Before using it in a monetized video or for a client, confirm the commercial-use question with Mastershot, because the official pages do not answer it.

## Honest verdict

Mastershot Studio's free plan is the most generous free editing offer I have checked in this series: the things other editors charge for — 4K export, no watermark, subtitles, background removal, pause removal — are all free here, with no credit card, no credits, and no time limit. For a creator who already has footage, whether shot on a camera or generated by an AI tool, and needs to cut it into a clean vertical video, that is a genuinely useful free tier.

The limits are equally real. Mastershot Studio is an editor, not a generator — it cannot make a video from a text prompt, so it is the second step in a creator's workflow, not the first. It needs a computer; there is no phone version of Studio. Commercial use is not verified on the official pages, so do not build a monetized or client workflow on the free plan until that is confirmed. And the account question is answered two different ways on the same page, so expect to sign in.

Use Mastershot Studio free if you want to edit your own clips into finished vertical videos with subtitles, music, and clean cuts, and export in up to 4K with no watermark, without paying anything or handing over a card. Pay for Pro at $7 a month or $70 a year only if you want cloud storage with shareable links — the editor itself does not change. Skip it if you need an AI video generator, a phone-only workflow, or a confirmed commercial licence — none of those are what this free plan promises.
